## 中文讲解

### 开场

这里是每日前沿文章。今天讲一篇浙江大学和上海人工智能实验室等单位的论文，名字叫 JVT，全称 Just Video Transformer，意思是只用一个视频 Transformer。

它问的问题很直接，生成视频，能不能不要自编码器，不要各种附加模块，就用一个结构简单的 Transformer，直接在像素上做扩散。

### 问题

先说背景。现在主流的视频生成是潜空间扩散，先用一个单独训练好的自编码器，把视频压缩成紧凑的表示，再在这个压缩空间里去噪。

这样做效率高，但作者指出，模型也被自编码器限制住了，比如重建有误差，训练和推理有额外成本，想同时生成视频和别的模态也不方便。

图像领域已经有工作回到像素空间，做出了有竞争力的质量。视频这边，作者说还是潜空间 DiT 占主导，DiT 就是扩散 Transformer。

### 以前的方法

那直接在像素上生成视频的方法呢。以前基本要靠特殊结构，要么保留 U-Net 式的跳跃连接，要么分好几级逐步生成。

这些设计有效，但作者认为，它们往往增加结构和训练上的复杂度，可能限制效率和扩展性。

最近的图像模型 JiT 证明，一个大 patch 的简单 Transformer，就能做出和潜空间扩散有竞争力的高分辨率图像。JVT 想回答的是，这条路在视频上能走多远。

### 核心想法

JVT 的核心想法就是做减法。不要预训练自编码器，不要细节增强模块，不要级联，也不要跳跃连接。作者称，这是第一个建立在这种简单 DiT 式主干上的像素空间视频扩散模型。

这张结构图基本就是全部。带噪的视频切成 patch，变成 token，经过一串 Transformer 模块，再用一个线性层还原成视频。

它还有一个好处。既然直接在像素上工作，深度这样和像素一一对齐的信息，拼到同一个网格上，就能和画面一起生成。

### 方法

先看预测目标。JVT 沿用 JiT 的做法，网络不预测噪声，而是直接预测干净的视频，损失仍然按速度来算，这是 rectified flow 的标准形式。

第一个设计问题是 patch embedding，也就是怎么把原始视频切块变成 token。在像素空间模型里，它要承担潜空间模型里自编码器做的下采样。

标准做法叫 tubelet，用一个三维卷积一步到位。作者还比较了先空间后时间的 patch-first，和先时间后空间的 frame-first，并且加上通道瓶颈，就是先压到很窄的维度，再展开。

结果在表 2。作者最后选的是 patch-first，瓶颈宽度 32，FVD 从普通 tubelet 的 145.0 降到 135.0。FVD 衡量生成视频和真实视频的分布差距，越低越好。

这个嵌入模块本身的计算量，从 7.248 GFLOPs 降到 0.503。不过它在整个模型里占比本来就小，整个去噪网络的计算量，只从 1.089 降到 1.082 TFLOPs。

第二个设计是 register token，可以理解成额外加的辅助 token，不对应任何画面。JVT 在前 9 个模块之后插入 32 个，用完再丢掉。

它的用处体现在采样阶段，叫 register guidance。同一个输入，分别算带 register 和不带 register 的预测，再沿着两者的差往前推一步。和原始做法不同，JVT 训练时不随机丢弃 register，作者发现这样依然有效。

表 3 说明了关键点。在默认的 50 步采样下，只加 register、不加这个引导，FVD 是 137.9，并不比不加 register 的 136.5 更好。加上引导，才降到 129.2。

第三个设计是条件注入。作者测试了去掉 DiT 标准的 AdaLN，也就是自适应层归一化，改用 register 传递类别信息，FVD 变差到 156.3，所以还是保留了 AdaLN。

最后是联合生成。把 RGB 和视差按通道拼起来，视差是深度的一种换算形式，一共四个通道，一起去噪。生成后把视差换回深度，结合相机参数，就能得到一串彩色点云，推理时不需要额外的重建模型。

### 实验和效果

实验设置。主实验用 UCF-101、FaceForensics、SkyTimelapse 和 Taichi-HD 四个数据集，视频是 16 帧，256 乘 256 分辨率，用 2048 个样本算 FVD。模型有 28 层，大约 6.79 亿参数。

结果在表 1。UCF-101 的类别条件生成，主要基线 Latte 的 FVD 是 477.97，JVT 是 136.5，加上 register guidance 是 129.2。

无条件生成上，FaceForensics 从 Latte 的 34.00 到 JVT 的 32.86，SkyTimelapse 从 59.82 到 46.58，Taichi-HD 从 159.60 到 119.1。论文称 JVT 好于表里所有对比方法，不过其他方法的分数，是从已有论文里引用的。

register guidance 的效果要看数据集。UCF-101 无条件生成从 413.5 降到 215.9，但另外三个数据集没有明显收益，SkyTimelapse 和 Taichi-HD 还略差一点。作者推测，这和 UCF-101 语义更丰富有关。

速度方面，在一张 NVIDIA 4090 上，不带 register，50 步采样加 classifier-free guidance，生成一段 16 帧视频大约 1.14 秒。

再看相机控制。给定第一帧和相机轨迹，在 RealEstate10K 上生成 8 帧视频。表 5 里，只生成 RGB 的 JVT，FVD 是 27.0，低于 DFoT 的 35.3。

同时生成视差之后，FVD 只升到 27.5，而 5 度阈值下的相机平移准确率 RTA，从 72.4 提高到 79.5，是表里最高的。

### 和以前方法的对比

不过，主表之外，附录里的对比没有那么一边倒。表 13 里，用了图像视频联合训练的方法，在三个无条件数据集上都比 JVT 好，比如 FrameDiT 在 FaceForensics 上是 16.6，JVT 是 32.86。作者说明，JVT 这里没有用联合训练，这个策略也可以加进来。

表 14 和自回归加扩散的混合方法比。UCF-101 类别条件生成上，FAR-XL 是 108，ACDiT-XL 是 111，都低于 JVT 的 136.5。无条件生成上，JVT 的 215.9 好于 FAR-XL 的 279。

相机控制的优势也要分开看。只生成 RGB 的 JVT，RTA 是 72.4，低于 DFoT 的 77.7。加上视差之后是 79.5，领先 DFoT 1.8 个百分点。

我的判断是，这篇的价值不在于拿下了视频生成的最好成绩，而在于证明这么简单的结构也能做到这个水平。对比时要注意，主要基线 Latte 一旦用上联合训练，在三个无条件数据集上就超过了 JVT。

### 局限

局限方面，先看实验范围。主实验是 16 帧、256 分辨率，相机实验只有 8 帧，任务是类别条件、无条件和相机控制，论文没有做文本生成视频。

作者自己指出一个关键局限，这类相机控制模型能比较可靠地跟随旋转，但复现相机平移要差得多。JVT 在 5 度阈值下，旋转准确率是 99.9，平移是 79.5。

另外，RealEstate10K 没有稠密深度标注，训练用的深度来自冻结的 Pi3X 模型，评测相机轨迹时，用的也是 Pi3X 估计的位姿。

register guidance 也不是免费的。每步要多算一次网络，而且主要在 UCF-101 上有收益，所以作者的最终配置只在这个数据集上使用 register。

### 未来方向

未来方向，作者希望把像素空间视频生成扩展到更大的模型、更多的数据集、更长的视频和更高的分辨率。

我的判断是，最大的问题是这条路能不能撑到高分辨率长视频。论文目前只验证到 16 帧、256 分辨率，也没有给出和潜空间模型的速度对比。

我的判断是，更值得关注的是联合生成。同一个网络一次输出画面和几何，对需要显式三维信息的世界模型很有吸引力，不过几何质量论文只给了可视化结果，没有做定量评估。

## English walkthrough

### Intro

This is Daily Frontier Papers. Today's paper comes from Zhejiang University, Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborators. It's called JVT, short for Just Video Transformer, and the name is the argument.

Can you generate video with no autoencoder and no bolt-on modules, using one plain Transformer that runs diffusion directly on pixels?

### Problem

Some background first. Most video generators today use latent diffusion. A separately trained autoencoder squeezes the video into a compact representation, and denoising happens in that compressed space.

That's efficient, but the authors argue the autoencoder also sets the ceiling. It adds reconstruction error, it adds training and inference cost, and it makes generating video jointly with other modalities harder.

Image generation has already seen pixel-space models reach competitive quality. Video, the authors say, is still dominated by latent DiTs, meaning diffusion Transformers.

### Prior work

So what about video models that do work on pixels? They have mostly relied on special structure, either U-Net-style skip connections or several cascaded generation stages.

Those designs work, but the authors say they often add architectural and training complexity, which can limit efficiency and scalability.

Then an image model called JiT showed that a simple large-patch Transformer could be competitive with latent diffusion on high-resolution images. JVT asks how far that recipe goes for video.

### Key idea

The key idea is subtraction. No pretrained autoencoder, no detail modules, no cascades, no skip connections. The authors claim it's the first pixel-space video diffusion model built on a backbone this simple.

This diagram is nearly the whole model. A noisy video is cut into patches, the patches become tokens, the tokens pass through a stack of Transformer blocks, and one linear head turns them back into video.

There's a bonus. Because the model works on pixels, anything aligned pixel by pixel, like depth, can sit on the same grid and be generated together with the picture.

### Method

Start with the prediction target. Following JiT, the network doesn't predict noise. It predicts the clean video directly, while the loss is still measured on velocity, the standard rectified-flow form.

The first design question is patch embedding, how raw video gets chopped into tokens. In a pixel-space model, this step has to do the downsampling that an autoencoder does in a latent model.

The standard option, called a tubelet, is a single 3D convolution. The authors also try patch-first, which handles space and then time, and frame-first, which does the reverse. They add a channel bottleneck too, squeezing features into a narrow width before expanding them.

Table 2 has the results. The authors choose patch-first with a bottleneck width of thirty-two, which takes FVD from one forty-five point zero for the plain tubelet to one thirty-five point zero. FVD measures the distance between generated and real video distributions, and lower is better.

The embedding module's own compute falls from seven point two four eight GFLOPs to zero point five zero three. But it was a tiny share of the model to begin with. The full denoiser only moves from one point zero eight nine to one point zero eight two TFLOPs.

The second design is register tokens, extra helper tokens that don't correspond to any part of the image. JVT inserts thirty-two of them after the first nine blocks and discards them at the end.

Their payoff comes at sampling time, through register guidance. For the same input, the model predicts once with registers and once without, then pushes further along the difference. Unlike the original recipe, JVT doesn't randomly drop registers in training, and the authors find guidance still works.

Table 3 shows why that matters. With the default fifty sampling steps, registers without guidance score one thirty-seven point nine, no better than one thirty-six point five with no registers at all. Turn guidance on and FVD drops to one twenty-nine point two.

The third design is conditioning. The authors try removing AdaLN, the adaptive layer normalization standard in DiTs, and passing class information through registers instead. FVD worsens to one fifty-six point three, so AdaLN stays.

Last is joint generation. RGB and disparity, which is a reparameterized form of depth, are stacked into four channels and denoised together. Convert disparity back to depth, apply the camera parameters, and you get a sequence of colored point clouds, with no separate reconstruction model at inference.

### Results

The setup. The main experiments use four datasets: UCF-101, FaceForensics, SkyTimelapse and Taichi-HD. Clips are sixteen frames at two fifty-six by two fifty-six, and FVD is computed on two thousand and forty-eight samples. The model has twenty-eight layers and about six hundred seventy-nine million parameters.

Table 1 has the headline numbers. For class-conditioned UCF-101, the main baseline, Latte, scores four seventy-seven point nine seven. JVT scores one thirty-six point five, and one twenty-nine point two with register guidance.

For unconditional generation, FaceForensics goes from Latte's thirty-four point zero zero to thirty-two point eight six, SkyTimelapse from fifty-nine point eight two to forty-six point five eight, and Taichi-HD from one fifty-nine point six zero to one nineteen point one. The paper says JVT beats every method in the table, but the other methods' scores are quoted from earlier papers.

Register guidance depends heavily on the dataset. Unconditional UCF-101 improves from four thirteen point five to two fifteen point nine, but the other three datasets see no clear gain, and SkyTimelapse and Taichi-HD get slightly worse. The authors suspect UCF-101's richer semantics explain the difference.

On speed, a single NVIDIA forty ninety generates a sixteen-frame clip in about one point one four seconds, using fifty steps with classifier-free guidance and no registers.

Next, camera control. Given a first frame and a camera path, the model generates eight frames on RealEstate10K. In Table 5, RGB-only JVT reaches an FVD of twenty-seven point zero, ahead of DFoT at thirty-five point three.

Generate disparity alongside RGB and FVD only rises to twenty-seven point five, while RTA, camera translation accuracy at a five-degree threshold, climbs from seventy-two point four to seventy-nine point five, the best in the table.

### Comparison

The appendix paints a less one-sided picture. In Table 13, methods that use joint image and video training beat JVT on all three unconditional datasets. On FaceForensics, FrameDiT scores sixteen point six against JVT's thirty-two point eight six. The authors note JVT doesn't use joint training here, and that it could be added.

Table 14 compares hybrids of autoregressive modeling and diffusion. On class-conditioned UCF-101, FAR-XL scores one hundred eight and ACDiT-XL one hundred eleven, both better than JVT's one thirty-six point five. On unconditional generation, JVT's two fifteen point nine beats FAR-XL's two seventy-nine.

The camera results need the same care. RGB-only JVT has an RTA of seventy-two point four, below DFoT's seventy-seven point seven. Adding disparity brings it to seventy-nine point five, one point eight points ahead of DFoT.

My read is that the value here isn't a new record for video generation. It's the evidence that such a simple architecture can get this far. Keep in mind that Latte, the main baseline, overtakes JVT on all three unconditional datasets once it uses joint training.

### Limitations

First, the scope of the experiments. The main runs are sixteen frames at two fifty-six resolution, the camera runs are only eight frames, and the tasks are class-conditioned, unconditional and camera-controlled. The paper doesn't test text-to-video.

The authors flag one key limitation themselves. Camera-conditioned generators like these follow rotations relatively reliably but reproduce translations much less accurately. At a five-degree threshold, JVT scores ninety-nine point nine on rotation and seventy-nine point five on translation.

Also, RealEstate10K has no dense depth annotations. The training depth comes from a frozen model called Pi3X, and the camera-following evaluation relies on poses estimated by Pi3X as well.

Register guidance isn't free either. It costs one extra network pass per step, and its gains are concentrated on UCF-101, which is why the authors' final configuration uses registers only on that dataset.

### Future work

Looking ahead, the authors hope to scale pixel-space video generation to larger models, more datasets, longer videos and higher resolutions.

My read is that the big open question is whether this approach holds up at high resolution and long duration. The paper only goes as far as sixteen frames at two fifty-six, and it offers no speed comparison against latent models.

My read is that joint generation is the part to watch. One network producing both pictures and geometry is attractive for world models that need explicit 3D, though the paper shows geometry quality only through visualizations, with no quantitative evaluation.
