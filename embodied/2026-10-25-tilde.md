## 中文讲解

### 开场

这里是每日前沿文章。今天讲一篇香港大学的论文，名字叫 TILDE，意思是让老师模型参与进来的蒸馏，对象是具身基础模型。

它想回答一个很实际的问题，一个做过大规模动作预训练的机器人大模型，它的本事，能不能不重做预训练，直接教给一个没做过动作预训练的新模型。

### 问题

先说背景。具身基础模型，包括 VLA，也就是看画面、听指令、直接输出机器人动作的模型。它们的泛化能力，很大程度上来自在大规模机器人数据上做的动作预训练，而这一步需要大量数据和算力，很多团队复现不起。

作者先做了一个预研，就是这张图。在 LIBERO 仿真里比较简单的 Spatial 和 Object 两组任务上，有没有动作预训练，成功率差不多，都在 96 以上。

但换到长流程任务 LIBERO-10，加了背景和初始姿态扰动的 LIBERO-Plus，还有真机上的拿饮料任务，差距就拉开了。真机拿饮料，π0.5 是 79.2，同样架构去掉动作预训练，只有 16.7。

所以问题就是，能不能把预训练带来的这部分能力，从老师模型转移到学生模型上，而不用再花一遍预训练的钱。

### 以前的方法

最直接的办法是策略蒸馏。离线蒸馏，就是在人类演示数据上，让学生模仿老师给出的动作。问题在于，监督只覆盖演示里出现过的状态，学生自己跑起来，小误差一累积，就会走到演示里没有的地方，在那里没人教过它。

第二条路是在线蒸馏，也就是 on-policy distillation，简称 OPD。让学生自己跑，老师在学生实际走到的状态上给出动作标签。但老师给的动作只用来当标签，并不真的执行，所以看不到照老师的纠正做下去，后面会走到什么状态。

第三条路是人在环，比如 HG-DAgger。人一直盯着机器人，出问题就用遥操作接管，这样能收集到从错误里恢复的轨迹。缺点是需要人持续监控、手动接管，数据规模很难做大。

### 核心想法

TILDE 的想法是，让老师模型自己来当那个接管的人。学生先跑，一个自动监控发现它卡住了，就让老师模型接手，把任务做完，这样拿到的恢复轨迹，不需要人参与。

于是学生同时从三种数据里学，人类演示，学生自己走到的状态，以及老师接管之后的恢复轨迹。

### 方法

先看数据收集，作者叫它 AutoDAgger，流程是这张图。监控用的是一个现成的机器人奖励模型，叫 Robometer，没有针对任务做微调。它从这一轮执行以来的画面里，均匀取最多 8 帧，加上任务指令，估计当前的任务进度和成功概率。

怎么判断学生失败了。执行管理器会记录最近一次有效执行的时间，进度比参考值高出 0.05 才算真的往前走，或者成功概率超过 0.5 也算。如果离上次有效执行已经过了一个时限，而且成功概率仍然不超过 0.5，就判定学生失败。

这个时限按任务和策略分别设定。比如叠抹布，学生的时限是 6 秒，老师是 14 秒。学生的时限短，可以早点接管，老师的时限长，给它留出从偏差状态里恢复的时间。

一旦判定失败，学生立刻停下，老师接管。老师执行时也用同样的方法监控，成功概率要连续一段时间都超过阈值，才确认任务完成。老师没救回来的整条丢掉，学生自己做成功的也丢掉，因为目标就是收集恢复数据。留下的是学生前半段加老师恢复段拼起来的轨迹。

再看训练。蒸馏直接在动作空间做，老师冻结，在每个状态预测一段动作，学生用它自己原本的训练目标去拟合这段动作。因为只对齐输出，不对齐内部表示，老师和学生可以是不同的架构。

损失按数据来源选。人类演示和老师恢复轨迹里，有真实执行过的动作，学生既模仿执行的动作，也拟合老师的预测。学生自己跑出来的状态，只用老师的预测做监督。蒸馏损失的权重设为 1。

还有一个实现细节。老师和学生都是 π0.5 架构时，直接对齐两者 flow-matching 的速度预测，并且共用同样的噪声。架构不同时，就用老师完全去噪之后的动作当目标。

### 实验和效果

实验在真机上做，用的是 AgileX PiperX 的 7 自由度机械臂，三个任务。叠抹布，对折两次后放到左前方。拿笔，把一到三支笔放进杯子。拿饮料，把饮料放进纸盒。人类演示，叠抹布 200 条，另外两个各 100 条。

老师是 π0.5 在每个任务的演示上微调出来的。学生和 π0.5 架构相同，但没做动作预训练，视觉语言部分从 PaliGemma 初始化，动作专家从零开始训练。

评分方式是把任务拆成几个阶段，每个阶段给 0 分、0.5 分或 1 分，平均后乘以 100。测试次数，叠抹布 12 次，拿笔 27 次，拿饮料 30 次。几种方法共用同一批 AutoDAgger 数据，每个任务 30 条成功恢复的轨迹。

主结果在这张图。TILDE 在三个任务上分别是 63.9、80.6 和 69.2，三个任务都超过所有学生基线。只做行为克隆的基线是 20.8、37.0 和 16.7，TILDE 大约是它的 3.07 倍、2.18 倍和 4.14 倍。作为参考，老师是 87.5、87.0 和 79.2。

中间几档也很清楚。离线蒸馏是 27.8、45.4 和 26.7，只比行为克隆高一点。OPD 是 43.1、58.3 和 40.0，又往上走了一截。

专门测恢复能力的实验在表 1。作者在叠抹布任务上预设了 12 个出错状态，看模型能不能救回来。行为克隆救回 4 个，离线蒸馏和 OPD 各 5 个，TILDE 是 8 个，老师是 9 个。

消融在图 4。从离线蒸馏出发，单独加上学生状态的老师监督，或者单独加上恢复轨迹，三个任务都有提升，两样都加，分数最高。

数据量的影响在表 2。叠抹布任务上，AutoDAgger 数据从 0 条加到 30、60、90 条，分数从 27.8 涨到 63.9、65.3 和 77.8，老师是 87.5。

数据质量的对比在表 3。同样多加 30 条，一种是人类演示，一种是 AutoDAgger 数据。开着蒸馏时，多加人类演示，三个任务是 29.2、54.6 和 48.3，加 AutoDAgger 数据是 63.9、80.6 和 69.2。作者的解释是，人类演示通常是一路走向成功的轨迹，很少包含学生自己会走到的偏差状态。

最后是跨架构，在表 5。在 LIBERO 仿真里，学生换成没做动作预训练的 SmolVLA，老师还是 π0.5。四组任务的平均成功率，学生本身是 81.2，离线蒸馏 83.5，TILDE 是 89.6。长流程的 LIBERO-10，从 64.6 提到 79.6。

### 和以前方法的对比

再看和人在环方法 HG-DAgger 的正面对比，在表 4。接管之后完成一次恢复的时间，叠抹布，AutoDAgger 是 21.5 秒，人是 54.4 秒，快 2.53 倍，拿笔快 2.23 倍，拿饮料快 2.60 倍。

但恢复成功率并不占优。叠抹布 73.3 对 76.7，拿笔 66.7 对 76.7，拿饮料都是 90。开着蒸馏时的最终分数，叠抹布 63.9 对 65.3，拿笔都是 80.6，拿饮料 69.2 对 63.3。作者还指出，自动数据加到 60 条，叠抹布能到 65.3，和人在环 30 条的结果持平。

监控本身靠不靠得住，作者也测了。在 LIBERO 上每组任务收集 200 条 SmolVLA 的运行记录，判断成败的 F1 在 0.873 到 0.994 之间，最难的 LIBERO-10 召回率最低，是 0.841。真机上测了 20 条，准确率 85%。

我的判断是，这篇最有价值的地方，是把人盯着、人接管这一步，换成了奖励模型盯着、老师模型接管。恢复数据以前要靠人一条条遥操作，现在可以自动、批量地收集。

我的判断是，也要看清楚它省的是什么。主实验里学生和老师是同一个架构，省掉的是动作预训练，不是推理成本。而且收集数据时，仍然需要一个能干活的老师在真机上跑。

### 局限

作者自己列了三条局限。第一，只做了一轮 AutoDAgger 收集再训练，没有在更新学生之后继续迭代收集。

第二，整个流程依赖监控判断准确，也依赖老师能把局面救回来。监控判错，或者出错状态超出了老师的能力，收集到的数据质量和效率都会下降。

第三，评测范围有限，真机任务、仿真基准和师生架构的组合都不多。另外从附录表 12 能看到，真机测试次数少，叠抹布只测了 12 次，TILDE 的 63.9 分，96% 置信区间是 38.9 到 87.5，范围很宽。

### 未来方向

作者计划把 TILDE 扩展到更多任务、更多机器人本体和不同的策略家族，看学生在持续自动收集和蒸馏之下，能多接近预训练大模型。表 2 里数据越多分数越高的趋势，也支持继续加数据。

我的判断是，如果这条路走得通，预训练好的 VLA 就可以当成一个可复用的老师，新架构不必各自重做一遍动作预训练。真正的检验，是多轮迭代之后学生能不能追平老师，以及换成更小、更快的学生时，效果还剩多少。

## English walkthrough

### Intro

This is Daily Frontier Papers. Today's paper comes from the University of Hong Kong. It's called TILDE, short for teacher-in-the-loop distillation from embodied foundation models.

The question is a practical one. A big robot model has gone through expensive large-scale action pretraining. Can you hand its skills to a new model that never had that pretraining, without paying for it all over again?

### Problem

Some background. Embodied foundation models include VLAs, vision-language-action models that look at camera images, read an instruction, and output robot actions directly. Much of their robustness comes from action pretraining on large robot datasets, and that takes data and compute most teams don't have.

The authors start with a pilot study, shown in this figure. On the easier LIBERO simulation suites, Spatial and Object, models with and without action pretraining look about the same, all above ninety-six.

But on the long-horizon LIBERO-10 suite, on LIBERO-Plus with background and initial-pose perturbations, and on a real-robot task of picking up a beverage, the gap opens up. On the real beverage task, pi zero point five scores seventy-nine point two. The same architecture without action pretraining scores sixteen point seven.

So the question becomes: can that extra capability be transferred from a pretrained teacher to a student, without repeating the pretraining?

### Prior work

The obvious tool is policy distillation. Offline distillation has the student imitate the teacher's actions on the human demonstration data. The catch is that supervision only covers states that appear in the demonstrations. Once the student runs on its own, small errors compound, it drifts somewhere the demos never went, and nobody taught it what to do there.

The second option is on-policy distillation, or OPD. The student runs, and the teacher labels the states the student actually visits. But the teacher's actions are only used as labels, never executed. So you never see where the robot would end up if it actually followed the teacher's correction.

The third option is human-in-the-loop, like HG-DAgger. A person watches the robot and takes over by teleoperation when something goes wrong, which gives you real recovery trajectories. The cost is a human watching and stepping in the whole time, and that doesn't scale.

### Key idea

TILDE's idea is to let the teacher model be the person who takes over. The student runs, an automatic monitor notices it's stuck, and the teacher model takes control and finishes the task. You get recovery trajectories with no human involved.

The student then learns from three kinds of data at once: human demonstrations, the states the student itself visits, and the teacher's recovery trajectories.

### Method

Data collection first. The authors call it AutoDAgger, and this is the pipeline. The monitor is an off-the-shelf robot reward model called Robometer, with no task-specific finetuning. It looks at up to eight frames, evenly spaced across the run so far, plus the instruction, and estimates task progress and the probability of success.

How does it decide the student has failed? The execution manager tracks the last moment of valid execution. Progress only counts if it beats the reference by zero point zero five, and a success probability above zero point five also counts. If too much time passes since the last valid moment, and success probability is still at or below zero point five, the student is declared failed.

That time limit is set per task and per policy. For dishcloth folding, it's six seconds for the student and fourteen for the teacher. A short limit for the student means an early takeover. A long one for the teacher gives it room to recover from wherever the student left it.

Once a failure is detected, the student stops and the teacher takes over. The teacher is monitored the same way, and success is only confirmed after the success prediction stays above the threshold for a sustained stretch. If the teacher fails, the episode is thrown away. Episodes the student finishes on its own are also thrown away, since the point is to collect recoveries. What's kept is the student's segment stitched to the teacher's recovery.

Now training. Distillation happens directly in action space. The frozen teacher predicts an action chunk at each state, and the student fits it using its own usual training objective. Because only outputs are matched, not internal representations, teacher and student can have different architectures.

The loss depends on where a sample came from. Demonstrations and teacher recoveries contain actions that were actually executed, so the student imitates those and also fits the teacher's prediction. On states from the student's own rollouts, it learns from the teacher's prediction only. The distillation weight is set to one.

One implementation detail. When both are pi zero point five models, the student matches the teacher's flow-matching velocity predictions using shared noise. When the architectures differ, the target is the teacher's fully denoised action.

### Results

The experiments run on real AgileX PiperX arms with seven degrees of freedom, on three tasks. Fold a dishcloth in half twice and place it front left. Put one to three pens into a cup. Put a beverage into a paper box. There are two hundred human demonstrations for the dishcloth and a hundred for each of the others.

The teacher is pi zero point five finetuned on each task's demonstrations. The student has the same architecture but no action pretraining: its vision-language backbone starts from PaliGemma, and its action expert is trained from scratch.

Scoring splits each task into stages worth zero, one half, or one, averaged and multiplied by a hundred. There are twelve test trials for the dishcloth, twenty-seven for pens, and thirty for the beverage. All methods share the same pool of AutoDAgger data, thirty successfully recovered episodes per task.

The main results are in this figure. TILDE scores sixty-three point nine, eighty point six and sixty-nine point two on the three tasks, beating every student baseline on all three. Plain behavior cloning scores twenty point eight, thirty-seven and sixteen point seven, so TILDE is roughly three point one, two point two, and four point one times higher. For reference, the teacher scores eighty-seven point five, eighty-seven and seventy-nine point two.

The steps in between are clear too. Offline distillation gets twenty-seven point eight, forty-five point four and twenty-six point seven, only a bit above behavior cloning. OPD gets forty-three point one, fifty-eight point three and forty, another step up.

Table one tests recovery directly. The authors set up twelve error states in the dishcloth task and check whether each policy can still finish. Behavior cloning recovers four, offline distillation and OPD five each, TILDE eight, and the teacher nine.

The ablation is in Figure four. Starting from offline distillation, adding teacher supervision on student states, or adding the recovery trajectories, each helps on all three tasks. Adding both scores highest.

Table two looks at data scale. On the dishcloth, going from zero to thirty, sixty and ninety AutoDAgger episodes moves the score from twenty-seven point eight to sixty-three point nine, sixty-five point three and seventy-seven point eight. The teacher is at eighty-seven point five.

Table three asks about data quality. Add thirty more episodes, either human demonstrations or AutoDAgger data. With distillation on, extra human demos give twenty-nine point two, fifty-four point six and forty-eight point three. AutoDAgger data gives sixty-three point nine, eighty point six and sixty-nine point two. The authors' explanation is that human demos usually go straight to success, so they rarely contain the off-course states a student actually gets into.

Finally, cross-architecture transfer, in Table five. In LIBERO simulation, the student becomes SmolVLA without action pretraining, and the teacher is still pi zero point five. Average success across the four suites goes from eighty-one point two for the student alone, to eighty-three point five with offline distillation, to eighty-nine point six with TILDE. On long-horizon LIBERO-10, it goes from sixty-four point six to seventy-nine point six.

### Comparison

Now the head-to-head with the human-in-the-loop method, HG-DAgger, in Table four. Time to complete a recovery after takeover on the dishcloth is twenty-one point five seconds for AutoDAgger versus fifty-four point four for a human, two point five three times faster. It's two point two three times faster on pens and two point six times faster on the beverage.

But recovery success isn't better. It's seventy-three point three versus seventy-six point seven on the dishcloth, sixty-six point seven versus seventy-six point seven on pens, and ninety for both on the beverage. Final scores with distillation: sixty-three point nine versus sixty-five point three on the dishcloth, eighty point six for both on pens, and sixty-nine point two versus sixty-three point three on the beverage. The authors add that with sixty automatic episodes, the dishcloth reaches sixty-five point three, matching human-in-the-loop with thirty.

They also check whether the monitor can be trusted. On LIBERO, with two hundred SmolVLA rollouts per suite, its success-or-failure F1 ranges from zero point eight seven three to zero point nine nine four, with the lowest recall, zero point eight four one, on the hardest suite, LIBERO-10. On twenty real-robot rollouts, it's eighty-five percent accurate.

My read is that the valuable move here is replacing a human watching and taking over with a reward model watching and a teacher model taking over. Recovery data used to need a person teleoperating every episode. Now it can be collected automatically, in bulk.

My read is also that you should be clear about what it saves. In the main experiments the student has the same architecture as the teacher. What's saved is action pretraining, not inference cost. And collecting the data still needs a working teacher running on the real robot.

### Limitations

The authors list three limitations. First, there's only one round of AutoDAgger collection followed by training. They didn't go back and collect again after updating the student.

Second, the whole pipeline depends on the monitor judging correctly and on the teacher being able to rescue the situation. Wrong intervention calls, or failure states beyond the teacher's ability, hurt data quality and efficiency.

Third, the evaluation is narrow: few real tasks, few simulation benchmarks, few teacher-student pairs. And the appendix, Table twelve, shows how small the real-robot test sets are. The dishcloth has only twelve trials, and TILDE's sixty-three point nine comes with a ninety-six percent confidence interval from thirty-eight point nine to eighty-seven point five. That's wide.

### Future work

The authors plan to extend TILDE to more tasks, more robot embodiments and other policy families, and to see how close a student can get to a pretrained foundation model with continued automatic collection and distillation. The upward trend with more data in Table two supports pushing further.

My read is that if this holds up, a pretrained VLA becomes a reusable teacher, and new architectures don't each need their own round of action pretraining. The real test is whether students catch up to the teacher after several rounds, and how much survives when the student is smaller and faster.
