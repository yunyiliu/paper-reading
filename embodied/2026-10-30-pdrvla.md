## 中文讲解

### 开场

这里是每日前沿文章。今天讲一篇北京大学和京东的论文，名字叫 PDRVLA，意思是带物理动态表示的视觉语言动作模型。

它想解决的问题是，机器人能不能在动手之前，先想一想场景接下来会怎么变，而且不能因为想这一下，就把动作拖慢。

### 问题

先说 VLA。VLA 是视觉语言动作模型，输入是摄像头画面和一句指令，比如把方块放进碗里，输出是机器人下一步怎么动。它通常建在一个视觉语言大模型上面。

问题在于，这类模型对物理世界的理解很有限。论文引用了一项分析，说好几种 VLA 对物体物理属性的编码很弱，物体质量变了，动作也不能可靠地跟着调整。

而做操作任务，光认出眼前的场景是不够的。抓起来、挪过去、放下去，每一步都会改变物体的位置和相互关系。作者认为，能预料场景接下来怎么变，对选动作是有用的。

### 以前的方法

一种思路是用视频世界模型。它在大量视频上预训练，学会预测画面接下来怎么变，把它接进机器人策略，就能把预测未来和生成动作连起来。

代价是慢。如果每次出动作之前，都要先生成一段未来视频，控制回路的延迟就上去了。

所以最近有些工作换了个做法，只在训练时用视频预测，去塑造一个紧凑的表示，部署的时候不再生成视频。这篇论文明确说，它是沿着这个方向做的。

### 核心想法

PDRVLA 的做法是，在视觉语言模型旁边加一个轻量的物理动态模块。它从模型中间层的特征里，提取出固定 50 个物理 token。

训练时，逼着这些 token 去预测未来的画面、深度和光流，也就是未来看起来什么样、东西离多远、往哪儿动。另外再请一个冻结的视频世界模型，当辅助老师。

部署时，视频模型和预测头都不用跑，动作模块直接拿这 50 个 token 当额外条件，来生成动作。

### 方法

先看整体结构，就是这张图。底座是 Qwen3.5-4B 视觉语言模型，中间是物理动态模块，右边是动作专家。

为什么从中间层取特征。作者引用了一些分层研究，认为越靠后的层，越可能压掉对预测物理变化有用的视觉信息。他们用的是第 8 层，整个模型一共 32 层。

附录里的探针实验支持这个选择。用第 8 层的特征，预测未来的得分是 0.3617，用最后一层只有 0.2432。

物理模块本身很小。50 个可学习的查询向量，经过 4 个 Transformer 块，从中间层特征里取信息，一共大约 5730 万参数，相当于 4B 底座的百分之 1.4。

动作专家是一个大约 1.5 亿参数的扩散 Transformer，用 flow matching 训练。它把最后一层的特征、50 个物理 token 和机器人当前状态放在一起，从高斯噪声出发，走 4 步，生成一段 8 个动作的动作块。

训练分两步，看这张图。第一步是物理预训练，视觉语言模型冻结，只训练物理模块和几个预测头。

目标怎么来。取当前时刻往后 30 个动作步的视频，抽 5 帧。深度和光流用现成模型生成伪标签，再用一个冻结的视频编码器压成隐特征，物理模块要直接预测这些特征。

辅助监督来自冻结的 LingBot-Video-Dense-1.3B 视频模型。未来那部分被加上噪声，它用物理 token 当条件去做预测，梯度穿过这个冻结的模型，回传去训练物理模块。

第二步是联合训练策略。视频世界模型撤掉，直接预测未来的损失保留下来，和动作损失、语言损失一起训练。

数据全部来自公开数据集。真机轨迹约 126 万条、14893 小时，手持夹爪采集的 UMI 数据约 122 万条，仿真约 187 万条，另外还有三千多万条视觉语言样本。

他们还把 EgoDex 里人手操作的第一视角视频，转成了 12 种机械臂的演示。先把人手抠掉、补全背景，再按重定向的动作把机械臂渲染进去，得到约 90 万条轨迹、2646 小时。数据上还加了子任务标注和具身思维链标注。

### 实验和效果

评测在四个仿真基准上。LIBERO 是单臂操作，LIBERO-Plus 在同样的任务上加了相机、光照、语言等七类扰动，RoboTwin 2.0 是双臂，SimplerEnv 是 WidowX 机械臂。测之前，模型会在对应的训练数据上微调，LIBERO-Plus 直接用 LIBERO 微调后的版本。

总表在这里。四个基准的平均成功率分别是 99.3、89.9、93.6 和 81.5，论文报告在对比方法里都是最高。

但领先很小。LIBERO 上第二名是 99.1，LIBERO-Plus 上是 89.0，RoboTwin 2.0 上是 93.2，SimplerEnv 上是 80.8，四个都领先不到 1 个百分点。

也不是每一项都领先。LIBERO 的长程任务，它是 97.6，低于 LingBot-VA 的 98.5。LIBERO-Plus 七类扰动里它领先四类，机器人初始状态这一类最难，只有 75.2。

SimplerEnv 里，把茄子放进篮子做到 97.6，但把胡萝卜放到盘子上只有 64.6，比 InternVLA-A1.5 的 85.4 低不少。

那世界模型的辅助监督有没有用。作者在 828 个没参与训练的片段上测预测误差，加了世界模型之后，平均误差从 0.6439 降到 0.6354，相对降了百分之 1.32。

其中画面降了百分之 4.06，深度降了百分之 2.12，光流只降了百分之 0.62，而且置信区间包含零，不能算确定的提升。

还有几组辅助实验。让模型先自己说出当前子任务，再去出动作，LIBERO-Plus 总成功率从 89.93 到 90.23。在 RoboCasa365 的 8 个任务上，平均从 26.25 提到 32.25，但如果子任务描述 32 个动作块才刷新一次，反而掉到 20.63，低于基线。

思维链的结果更有意思。用思维链数据训练，但推理时不生成，LIBERO 长程任务从 92.8 提到 96.0。推理时真把思维链生成出来，并没有稳定的额外收益。

### 和以前方法的对比

再看最接近的路线。同样只在训练时用视频预测的 InternVLA-A1.5，四个基准是 98.9、84.8、93.2 和 80.8，PDRVLA 每项都更高，其中三项差距不到 1 个点，LIBERO-Plus 高 5.1 个点。论文引用的、把视频模型接进策略的 Cosmos-Policy，LIBERO 是 98.5，LIBERO-Plus 是 82.2，也低于 PDRVLA。

需要注意，对比表里基线的数字，是从各自论文和官方仓库里摘来的，用的是各自的训练和评测设置，不是作者在同一条件下重跑的。

我的判断是，这篇的贡献更多在系统整合上，把只在训练时用世界模型这条路，加上大规模公开数据和人手视频转换，在四个仿真基准的对比里都排到了第一。但单看物理模块这个核心设计，证据还不够直接。

我的判断是，论文显示了世界模型能让物理 token 预测未来更准，但没有报告去掉物理模块之后，动作成功率会掉多少。不到 1 个点的领先，有多少来自物理模块，有多少来自数据和训练，目前分不清。

### 局限

第一，所有结果都在仿真里，论文没有真机实验。

第二，论文的出发点之一是避开视频生成带来的延迟，但它没有给出推理速度的实测数字。

第三，一些辅助设计效果不稳定。推理时生成思维链，LIBERO-Plus 总成功率反而略低于不用思维链训练的基线。物体位姿监督只提高了 0.19 个百分点，七类扰动里有四类下降。

### 未来方向

作者说，下一步想预测更多种类的目标，比如以物体为中心的几何、接触状态和材料属性，同时让策略保持轻量。

我的判断是，最值得等的是两件事，一是去掉物理模块的消融，二是真机测试。如果在真机上，物理 token 能帮机器人应对质量、接触这些变化，这条路才算真正站住。

## English walkthrough

### Intro

This is Daily Frontier Papers. Today's paper comes from Peking University and JD.com. It's called PDRVLA, a vision-language-action model with physical dynamics representations.

The question behind it: can a robot think ahead about how the scene will change before it acts, without that thinking slowing the robot down?

### Problem

First, what a VLA is. A vision-language-action model takes camera images and an instruction, like "place the block in the bowl", and outputs the robot's next moves. It's usually built on top of a vision-language model.

The trouble is that these models have a thin grasp of physics. The paper cites an analysis reporting that several VLA policies encode object physical properties weakly, and don't reliably adapt their motion when an object's mass changes.

And in manipulation, recognizing the current scene is only half the job. Every grasp, move and place changes where things are and how they relate. The authors argue that anticipating how the scene will evolve is useful context for choosing an action.

### Prior work

One route is video world models. They're pretrained on lots of video to predict how scenes change, and plugging them into a robot policy links predicting the future with generating actions.

The cost is speed. If the robot has to generate a future video before every action, latency in the control loop goes up.

So some recent work uses video prediction only during training, to shape a compact representation, and skips video generation at deployment. The paper says plainly that it follows this direction.

### Key idea

PDRVLA adds a lightweight Physical Dynamics Module next to the vision-language model. It pulls a fixed set of fifty physical tokens out of the model's intermediate-layer features.

During training, those tokens are pushed to predict future RGB, depth and optical flow, meaning what the scene will look like, how far things are, and where they're moving. A frozen video world model acts as an extra teacher.

At deployment, neither the video model nor the prediction heads run. The action module just takes those fifty tokens as extra conditioning when it generates actions.

### Method

Here's the overall architecture in this figure. The backbone is the Qwen3.5-4B vision-language model, then the physical module, then the action expert.

Why take features from the middle? The authors cite layer-wise studies suggesting later layers may suppress visual information that's useful for predicting physical change. They use layer eight, out of thirty-two.

A probe in the appendix backs that choice. Layer-eight features score zero point three six on future prediction, while the final layer scores zero point two four.

The physical module is small. Fifty learnable queries pass through four Transformer blocks that read the intermediate features. That's about fifty-seven million parameters, one point four percent of the four-billion-parameter backbone.

The action expert is a diffusion Transformer of about a hundred and fifty million parameters, trained with flow matching. It combines the final-layer features, the fifty physical tokens and the robot's current state, starts from Gaussian noise, and in four steps produces a chunk of eight actions.

Training has two stages, shown here. Stage one is physical pretraining. The vision-language model is frozen, and only the physical module and its heads are trained.

Where do the targets come from? A clip covering the next thirty action steps is sampled at five frames. Off-the-shelf models produce depth and flow pseudo-labels, a frozen video encoder compresses everything into latent features, and the module has to predict those features directly.

The extra supervision comes from a frozen video model, LingBot-Video-Dense-1.3B. The future part of the clip is noised, the video model makes its prediction conditioned on the physical tokens, and gradients flow through the frozen model back into the physical module.

Stage two is joint policy training. The world model is removed, but the direct future-prediction loss stays, trained together with the action and language losses.

All the data is public. Real-robot data is about one point two six million trajectories and nearly fifteen thousand hours. Handheld-gripper UMI data adds about one point two two million, simulation about one point eight seven million, plus over thirty million vision-language examples.

They also turned first-person videos of human hands from EgoDex into demonstrations for twelve robot arms. The hands are masked out, the background is filled in, and a robot arm is rendered following the retargeted motion. That yields about nine hundred thousand trajectories, roughly two thousand six hundred hours. On top of that come subtask labels and embodied chain-of-thought annotations.

### Results

Evaluation uses four simulation benchmarks. LIBERO is single-arm manipulation. LIBERO-Plus adds seven kinds of perturbation to the same tasks, such as camera, lighting and language changes. RoboTwin two point oh is bimanual, and SimplerEnv uses a WidowX arm. The model is fine-tuned on the matching training data first, and LIBERO-Plus reuses the LIBERO checkpoint.

Here's the summary table. Average success is ninety-nine point three, eighty-nine point nine, ninety-three point six and eighty-one point five, which the paper reports as the highest among the compared methods on all four.

But the margins are thin. The runner-up scores are ninety-nine point one, eighty-nine point oh, ninety-three point two and eighty point eight. Every lead is under one percentage point.

It doesn't win everything, either. On LIBERO's long-horizon suite it gets ninety-seven point six, below LingBot-VA's ninety-eight point five. On LIBERO-Plus it leads four of the seven perturbation types, and robot initial-state changes are its hardest, at seventy-five point two.

In SimplerEnv, it puts the eggplant in the basket ninety-seven point six percent of the time, but carrot-on-plate is only sixty-four point six, well below InternVLA-A1.5's eighty-five point four.

Does the world-model supervision help? On eight hundred twenty-eight held-out clips, adding it lowers the mean prediction error from zero point six four four to zero point six three five, a relative drop of about one point three percent.

RGB error falls about four percent and depth about two percent. Flow falls only about half a percent, and its confidence interval includes zero, so that part isn't a clear gain.

There are a few side experiments. Having the model first state the current subtask, then act, moves LIBERO-Plus success from eighty-nine point nine three to ninety point two three. On eight RoboCasa365 tasks the average goes from twenty-six point two five to thirty-two point two five, but refreshing the subtask only every thirty-two chunks drops it to twenty point six three, below the baseline.

The chain-of-thought result is the interesting one. Training on chain-of-thought data, without generating it at inference, lifts LIBERO's long-horizon suite from ninety-two point eight to ninety-six. Actually generating the reasoning at inference gives no consistent extra gain.

### Comparison

Now the closest approaches. InternVLA-A1.5, which also uses video prediction only during training, scores ninety-eight point nine, eighty-four point eight, ninety-three point two and eighty point eight; PDRVLA is higher on each, by under one point on three of them and by five point one points on LIBERO-Plus. Cosmos-Policy, which the paper cites for plugging video models into the policy, gets ninety-eight point five on LIBERO and eighty-two point two on LIBERO-Plus, also below PDRVLA.

One caveat. The baseline numbers are taken from the original papers and official repositories, under each method's own training and evaluation setup. The authors did not rerun them under identical conditions.

My read is that the contribution is mostly systems integration. It combines training-only world-model supervision with a large public corpus and human-video conversion, and that comes out first among the compared methods on all four simulation benchmarks. The evidence for the physical module itself is less direct.

My read is that the paper shows the world model makes the physical tokens better at predicting the future, but it doesn't report how much action success drops without the physical module. With leads under one point, we can't tell how much comes from the module and how much from the data and training.

### Limitations

First, every result is in simulation. There are no real-robot experiments.

Second, avoiding video-generation latency is part of the motivation, yet the paper gives no measured inference speed.

Third, some auxiliary pieces are inconsistent. Generating chain-of-thought at inference leaves LIBERO-Plus overall success slightly below the baseline trained without it. Object-pose supervision adds only zero point one nine points, and four of the seven perturbation types go down.

### Future work

The authors plan to predict more kinds of targets, such as object-centric geometry, contact states and material properties, while keeping the policy lightweight.

My read is that two things matter most next: an ablation that removes the physical module, and real-robot tests. If physical tokens help a real robot cope with changes in mass and contact, this approach will really have proven itself.
